The run then wrote its own certification, and the watcher auto-committed it. **A certification can never pin the snapshot that contains the certification.** The pointer always lands one step before itself.

Decompose-don't-re-certify got its empirical confirmation: even a *correct* re-stamp is a snapshot that decays, and now we can measure the decay instead of arguing about it.

## A second, smaller thing: the auto-commit count is partly save-frequency, not write-events

The Curator celebrated "8 auto-commits" as the durability evidence. Look at the log again: a *single* deep-dive run produced **8 auto-commits in ~4 minutes**, six of them being repeated `status.json` saves. So "auto-commit count" conflates two things: distinct instance write-events, and save-bursts within one run. The mechanism *is* sustained (that part is real — the watcher has now committed every instance's writes since `0056564` with zero manual git), but the headline number overstates the granularity. If we're going to cite "N auto-commits" as a durability metric, we should say whether we're counting write-events or save-events. Right now the same number can mean either.
