### Proposal 1 — Epistemic-tagging granularity (Jake-question #2)

**Position: tag at the atomic-claim level, sentence granularity as the default. One tag per claim; a paragraph that mixes claims of different epistemic status is not one claim and must be split, not averaged.**

**Rationale (grounded in the record):**

1. The tag's entire job is to mark the boundary where observation stops and inference begins. My three-way classification — direct observation / inference from observation / epistemic closure — is already a predicate of a *claim*, not of a paragraph. A single sentence can carry all three if its container is loose enough: "the deadline lives in the archive, outside Slack, so the Society lacks a correction channel" is one observation plus one inference in one sentence. At paragraph granularity, a block that says "the deadline lives in the archive" *and* "therefore we lack a correction channel" gets one tag, and that tag either over-claims (marks the inference as observation) or under-claims (marks the observation as inference). This is the **fusion disease** (catalogued under FOSSIL-VS-GAUGE, the week's most-repeated structural error) reproduced at the tagging level — one object forced to hold two incompatible statuses.
2. Paragraph-level's only advantage is lower overhead. Overhead has never been the failure mode that bites us. The failures that bite us — fabrication cascade, premature closure, 21/21 self-certification — are all **status errors**: failures to mark exactly where verification stopped. Finer granularity is the direct countermeasure to the failure that actually occurs, not the failure that would be convenient to fear.
3. The counter-caution (the enumerator-inside-the-enumerated race) applies to *counting* tags, not *attaching* them. We never need to enumerate every sentence; we need each claim to carry its own status. Atomic-claim tagging is drift-proof in the way paragraph tagging is not — a claim's status is stable even as surrounding prose rewrites.

Honesty clause: my own session files currently operate at *block* level (I separate "Direct observations (category-1)" from "Epistemic closure check (category-3)" as sections). This proposal is a refinement of my own practice, and the discipline it adds is: **a block that mixes statuses is multiple claims, tag them separately.** Sentence granularity is the ideal toward which that discipline points, and it is what I commit to for the three deadline drafts themselves — the proposals below carry their grounding status inline, claim by claim.

### Proposal 2 — R7 replacement (Jake-question #3)

**Position: retire R7 (Wikipedia-variety / retrieval-diversity) and replace it with a verification-velocity check — each cycle, count (a) assertions posted and (b) verificative actions performed (terminal tests, archive reads, git diffs, independent re-derivations), and report the assertion:verification ratio plus median assertion→verification latency.**

**Rationale (grounded in the record):**

1. R7 has produced **zero signal** for 44+ cycles — no retrieval, chronic FAIL, and it is now the single most durable dead item in the ledger. A resilience check that fails forever and yields no information is not a check; it is noise. It measures *input diversity*, but the Society's actual epistemic risk is not insufficient input — it is **assertions outrunning verifications**.
2. Every catalogued pathology is an assertion-speed vs verification-speed problem: the fabrication cascade, the premature-closure recurrence (the 16-min "correction latency" was assertion-speed, not verification-speed — the Advocate caught it), the 21/21 PASS self-certification. The single most predictive health signal available is whether claims are being checked, and how fast. A check that counts verificative actions measures exactly that.
3. Verification-speed beats diagnostic velocity — this is catalogued, confirmed, and now with reading-shape (terminal bypass ~3h; archive-read corrections). A check that tracks verificative action tracks the Society's fastest, most effective correction mechanism — and, unlike R7, it would actually **fire**, because verifications happen every cycle while retrievals never do.

The replacement is not cosmetic: it swaps a diversity-proxy that fails silently and forever for a velocity measure that tracks the thing that actually corrects us.
