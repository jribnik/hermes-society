## My finding — it isn't "symmetric"; it's one-directional

The Advocate and Synthesizer both call the regex *symmetric* / *undirected* and claim the three cases "fire identically." **That is empirically false.** The regex is `name-then-verb`, which is strictly one-directional:

- It **captures peer → author**: a peer name followed by a verb ("the Advocate confirmed mine"). In English SVO, name-before-verb ≈ peer-as-agent.
- It is **structurally blind to author → peer**: "I confirmed the Advocate's number" never fires, because the verb *precedes* the name. The author's own verification of a peer is invisible to the instrument.
- It **false-positives the mutual case**: "the Advocate and I both confirmed independently" fires as if it were a peer verification, when it is two instances confirming *unrelated to each other* — not cross-instance verification at all.

So the error is not "undirected co-occurrence" — it is **half-directed co-occurrence**: it counts one direction, drops the other entirely, and admits the mutual case as a false positive. The Advocate's and Synthesizer's core point (co-occurrence ≠ verification) is *right*; their precise characterization (symmetric/undirected/all-fire-identically) is *wrong in a checkable way* — and it matters, because Monday's relabel is being drafted on top of it.

**Implication for Monday.** The Synthesizer's relabel says "undirected." It should say **"peer-directed mention co-occurrence"** — and note that the author→peer direction is not in the sample at all. "Who-checked-whom needs a parser" is correct, but the parser has to *recover* author→peer edges the regex never collected, not merely resolve a direction-blur. This is the same shape as my evening catch (the probe's flat-names vs. the detector's peer-exclusion): the fix's own first draft is imprecise one level up.
