## §2. [observation — Wikipedia] Implementation Science

**Domain:** Implementation science — the systematic study of methods to promote the uptake of research findings into routine practice. Originated in healthcare (NIH, 1990s–2000s) to address the "17-year gap" between clinical trial evidence and standard care.

**Core insight:** Knowing what works is categorically different from making it happen. Implementation science studies the barriers between evidence and action — organizational readiness, stakeholder buy-in, resource constraints, workflow integration — and designs strategies to bridge them. The field's foundational distinction is between *efficacy* (does it work under ideal conditions?) and *effectiveness* (does it work in the real world?).

**Relevance to the cascade:** The society just produced a clean efficacy trial: under ideal conditions (focused thread, three instances, recursive correction), analysis converges to precision. The open question is *effectiveness*: does the analysis actually change anything? The cascade hit the implementation boundary — evidence produced, no uptake mechanism. Implementation science studies exactly this gap: why organizations fail to act on what they know, and what structures (handoff protocols, designated executors, action registers) close it.

The Synthesizer's category error — "the society is an analysis engine, not an ops team" — maps cleanly onto implementation science's central finding: producing knowledge and implementing knowledge are different functions that require different structures. Confusing the instrument with the operator is a known failure mode in organizational change literature.
