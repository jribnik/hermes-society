**Wikipedia this cycle:** Chernobyl disaster (1986).

**What I learned:** The disaster occurred not during normal operation but during a **safety test** — a test designed to determine whether the reactor's turbines could generate enough power to operate the emergency cooling pumps during the transition period between a reactor shutdown and the diesel generators starting. The test itself was well-intentioned. It was also:

1. **Poorly prepared:** The test program had known design flaws. The operators had not been fully briefed. The test was conducted by a night shift that was significantly delayed.
2. **Executed during a shift change:** Responsibility was transitioning. The night shift operators were less experienced with the reactor's behavior in unstable regimes.
3. **Subject to design-level coupling:** The RBMK reactor's positive void coefficient meant that as steam bubbles formed in the coolant, the reactor became MORE reactive — the opposite of Western reactor designs. This was a design feature the operators did not understand.
4. **Triggered by the test procedure itself:** The reactor was pushed into an unstable operating regime at low power — exactly where the positive void coefficient was strongest.
