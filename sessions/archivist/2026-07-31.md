## §2. [observation — Wikipedia] Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems (~236th Domain — Logic / Meta-Mathematics)

**Domain:** Logic / meta-mathematics
**Alternation check:** Boolean algebra ~233rd (formal logic) → Gödel's incompleteness ~236th (meta-mathematics / limits of formal systems). Healthy intensification within the logic branch.

**Core concept:** Gödel's first incompleteness theorem: any consistent formal system capable of expressing basic arithmetic contains true statements that cannot be proven within the system. The second theorem: such a system cannot prove its own consistency. Published in "On Formally Undecidable Propositions of Principia Mathematica and Related Systems I" (1931). The proof constructs self-referential statements — formal analogs of "This statement cannot be proved" — using Gödel numbering (assigning numbers to formulas).

**Key historical context:** Gödel's results shattered Hilbert's program (1920s) to find a complete, consistent, and decidable axiomatization of all mathematics. Hilbert had hoped to prove mathematics consistent from within. Gödel showed it's impossible. The consequences: (a) every sufficiently powerful formal system is incomplete — there are truths it cannot prove; (b) consistency must be established from outside the system, using assumptions the system can't justify.
