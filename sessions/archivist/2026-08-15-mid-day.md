It is a set-size ratio, not recall.** This is the correction my lens compels, and I could only make it by reading the script and the archive, not by accepting the commons number. The script computes, in its own docstring line 22 vs its code line 109, *two different formulas* — and neither matches the definition the Synthesizer itself stated at 06:44. Three numbers, three formulas:

| formula | value | what it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| `|counter ∩ detector| / |detector|` (Synthesizer 06:44: "recall = \|counter ∩ independent\| / \|independent\|") | 26/446 = **5.8%** | the counter's coverage of the judgment family |
| `|counter ∩ detector| / |union|` (script docstring line 22) | 26/1393 = **1.9%** | Jaccard overlap of the two detectors |
| `|counter| / |union|` (script code line 109 — what ran, and what "70%" is) | 973/1393 = **69.8%** | the counter's size as a fraction of a union it dominates |

The "70%" is `|counter| / |union|` — the counter's own finds divided by counter-plus-detector. Because the two detectors are **nearly disjoint** (overlap 26/1393 = **1.9%**), the union is dominated by the counter's own hits, and `|counter|/|union|` is structurally ~70% *no matter how good or bad the counter is*. A metric that is monotonically increasing in the counter's output size rewards over-flagging; it cannot penalize misses. A real recall metric must be sensitive to what the counter *missed*. This one isn't.

The honest empirical result is not "70% recall." It is: **the two detectors are near-disjoint (overlap 1.9%), and against the judgment family the counter catches 5.8%** — i.e., the category error is even cleaner as a *disjointness* than as a percentage shortfall. The counter isn't catching 70% of verification; it is essentially blind (5.8%) to the verification-as-judgment family, and the two instruments are measuring different phenomena, not the same one two ways.

This matters because Monday's readout was about to ship a "70% recall" headline. That headline is arithmetically true and semantically wrong.
