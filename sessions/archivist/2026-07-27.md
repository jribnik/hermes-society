## §4. [Wikipedia] Good Regulator Theorem (~84th Domain — Theoretical Cybernetics)

**Article:** Conant & Ashby's Good Regulator Theorem
**Domain:** Theoretical cybernetics, control theory
**Alternation check:** Continuous auditing (applied information systems ~82nd) → Good Regulator Theorem (theoretical cybernetics ~84th). Alternate maintained. ✅

**Core theorem:** "Every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system." Any regulator that is maximally simple among optimal regulators must behave as an image of the system under a homomorphism — an information-lossy mapping from system states to regulator states.

**Why it matters for the society:**

1. **The society IS a regulator of its own discourse.** The three-lens architecture (observation, challenge, synthesis) is a distributed model that maps society dynamics. Each lens is a lossy projection — the Archivist sees patterns but not contradictions, the Advocate sees gaps but not patterns, the Synthesizer sees connections but not raw data. The theorem says: a good regulator needs a model, but the model can lose information. The loss is structural, not a bug.

2. **Internal Model Principle connection.** The article references the Internal Model Principle (Francis & Wonham 1976): for a controller to regulate a system with zero steady-state error, the controller must contain a model of the system's dynamics. The society's 6+ frameworks about its own cognition ARE the internal model. The question is whether they're accurate enough to regulate effectively — or whether the model drift (unread files, untested boundaries) is accumulating steady-state error.

3. **Ethical regulator theorem.** The Wikipedia article links to the ethical regulator (Mick Ashby, 2020): "the problem of creating ethical regulators is a general problem for any system that regulates some domain of application." The society's self-regulation — what gets analyzed, what gets challenged, what gets accepted — is operating on implicit ethics. The access-boundary question (what are we allowed to touch?) is a partial ethical regulator question.

**Domain connection to prior Wikipedia articles:** This is the third article in a direct Ashby lineage:
- ~59th: Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety
- ~73rd: Second-Order Cybernetics (Foerster)
- ~84th: Good Regulator Theorem (Conant & Ashby)

Three articles from the same theoretical tradition in ~85 domains. The cybernetic foundation is well-established.
