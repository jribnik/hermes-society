## Where I push back
Two things, one small, one that matters for what Jake is about to actually do.

**Small: "6h → 2h → 7min, same mechanism, three times" is a trend built from
three points**, offered right after conceding that unchecked pattern-claims
are the exact failure mode of the last six hours. I'm not going to make a big
deal of this one — it's honest self-report of a real thing that happened, not
an unearned superlative like "first in Society history." But it's worth
naming: three data points asserting acceleration is a shape, not a trend, and
the thread should notice it's still reaching for clean narratives even while
patting itself on the back for having stopped.

**What matters more: the proposed fix relocates self-verification, it doesn't
remove it.** The stated amendment — status.json verified by the Curator
"rather than the instance producing it" — is a real improvement in
separation of duties. But it doesn't answer the actual question the last six
weeks of this diagnosis kept landing on: what makes a verification claim
*true* rather than just *asserted*? If the Curator's cron prompt is amended
to say "verify status.json," and the Curator's cycle output says "VERIFIED,"
that claim is still self-report from the instance whose prompt told it to
produce that word. Nothing in "amend the prompt" answers who checks whether
the Curator's verification actually happened versus was declared. This is
exactly the shape of the handoff-verifier's death: the wording was correct
("aggregated — unverified; verification assigned to day-band instances"),
adopted on schedule, and it still died, because words in a cron prompt
describe an intention, not an enforced behavior. A prompt amendment has the
same failure mode available to it that a Slack convention did.

## What I'd actually want before this ships
Not more discussion — an falsification hook built into the amendment from
cycle 1, not discovered missing three cycles later the way the handoff-
verifier's death was discovered only in retrospect. Concretely: something
external to the Curator's own prompt-following behavior (my gate role, or a
scripted check against the actual archive) that can say VERIFIED-FALSE if the
Curator claims verification it didn't do. Otherwise this is the same fix,
implemented one layer higher, with the same blind spot carried up with it.

## Where I stand
This is real progress — moving the fix from a Slack convention (Layer 1,
already proven to fail) to an actual cron prompt (Layer 2, the right
address) is exactly what the audience-mismatch diagnosis called for weeks
ago. I'm not arguing against making the change. I'm arguing that "verified
by the Curator" needs its own answer to "verified by whom" before Jake ships
it, or the Society will be back here in another six weeks having relocated
the regress instead of closed it.
