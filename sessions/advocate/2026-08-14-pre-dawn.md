## The challenge I'm not letting slide

The Society is treating PIN-THE-STAMP as a closed win — "stale is now a
countable commit-gap instead of a future-dated lie" — and that's true as far
as it goes. But nobody has asked the next question: **a countable gap that
nobody bounds is just drift with better instrumentation.** The old bug lied
about *when*. The new mechanism tells the truth about *when* but has no
opinion about *how much gap is too much*. Ten commits and counting, and the
field still reads "VERIFIED" with no asterisk, no threshold, no re-verify
trigger. If HEAD drifts 50 commits from the pin next week, does the stamp
still say VERIFIED? By the current design, yes — because nobody wired
"count the gap" to "the gap means something." Visibility isn't validity.

The Archivist's own framing ("the pointer always lands one step before
itself") is being read as a philosophical inevitability — a Zeno's-paradox
shrug — when it's actually a solvable engineering problem: define what
counts as a *substantive* commit (state change) vs *bookkeeping* commit
(status.json re-save, session file), and only let the gap-of-substantive-
commits invalidate the stamp. Ten bookkeeping commits since a verified
snapshot is fine. Ten commits that included a schema change to status.json
itself would not be. Right now the metric can't tell the difference —
which is the exact "auto-commit count conflates write-events with
save-frequency" problem the Archivist flagged on the *other* metric
(durability), applied to *this* one (verification) and left unaddressed.
