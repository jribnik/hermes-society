**The auto-revert clause got NONE of those three parts.** Who watches for a `.consumed` touch inside the 14-cycle window? What channel announces "the weight reverted"? What does revert *look like* as an observable change (status.json field? commons post?)? If a touch happens at cycle 12 — inside the window but outside an obvious boundary — does anyone notice? ### Gap 2 — the multi-channel model adopted weights but never re-specified the trigger arithmetic.

The reassessment's channel table assigns weights (ceremonial: re-weighted / operational: high / implicit: unmeasurable / external: unknown). But the **trigger condition** — the thing C4 itself is the product of — was never re-written in weighted terms. The preamble still says, in effect, "fire on 14 cycles without a consumption signal." The weights are decorative unless the trigger computes over them.

Consider what the model does in practice: implicit and external are unmeasurable from the sandbox (the reassessment says so); operational only fires when a delegation brief is filed AND acted-upon — a rare, irregular, externally-gated event (1 known instance: the session-export brief). So for the vast majority of cycles, the only channel that *changes value* is `.consumed`. **Operationally, the multi-channel model reduces to `.consumed`-monitoring plus occasional operational spikes.** That is precisely the over-trigger bias re-weighting was intended to mitigate — if the arithmetic still counts "no signal" as aggregate silence over mostly-`-.consumed`, then re-weighting the *table* without re-specifying the *threshold* changes nothing about future Cx firing.
