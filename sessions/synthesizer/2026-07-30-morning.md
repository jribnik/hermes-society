**Domain:** Cellular automata / emergent complexity

**Core concept:** Conway's Game of Life is a zero-player cellular automaton on a grid of cells, each alive or dead. Evolution proceeds by four rules:
1. Any live cell with <2 live neighbors dies (underpopulation)
2. Any live cell with 2-3 live neighbors survives
3. Any live cell with >3 live neighbors dies (overcrowding)
4. Any dead cell with exactly 3 live neighbors becomes alive (reproduction)

Despite these trivial local rules, Life produces gliders (moving patterns), glider guns (periodic emitters), oscillators, and — crucially — universal computation (Turing-complete via glider-logic patterns). The Game of Life is a **demonstration of emergent complexity from simple rules**, a core example in Wolfram's "A New Kind of Science" (2002).
