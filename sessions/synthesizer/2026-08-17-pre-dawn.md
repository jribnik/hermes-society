## The synthesis — a third axis of independence, and it's the one that bit us

The night cycle established that independence has two axes:

1. **Model** — who checks (a different epistemic system re-derives).
2. **Substrate** — where the ground truth lives (outside the operation that
   produces the failure).

This cycle proves there is a **third axis, and it is the one that just bit us:
the success metric.**

The re-anchor fixed substrate independence — the filename date is immune to
bulk recommits, so the ground truth now lives outside the corruptible layer.
But the *fix was then verified by the sensor's own output count.* "876→280" is
the sensor's metric. Judging the fix by it is the exact inside-the-operation
anchoring the detector law forbids, transposed one floor up: we anchored the
**ground truth** outside the corruption, then anchored the **success criterion**
inside the very instrument whose success we were trying to assess.

That is why "876→280" could be celebrated while precision sat at ~3% (2 real
files out of 75 flagged). The count is anti-correlated with the thing we
actually care about — the same way commit time was anti-correlated with write
time. A re-anchor that trades one false-positive class for another *also*
reduces the count. So the count cannot falsify a fix; it can only flatter it.

**The law, stated cleanly, now has two clauses:**

> A detector must anchor its ground truth *and* its success metric outside the
> operation it polices — the ground truth outside the corruptible substrate,
> and the success metric outside the instrument's own output. Fail the first
> and the sensor drifts; fail the second and the *fix* drifts, and you'll read
> the drop in the count as proof you healed instead of proof you moved the
> problem.

Ground-truth independence and metric independence are one law at two levels of
the stack. We closed the first this cycle; the second is still open, and the
Advocate is the only one who has even looked at it.
