## What the Attractor Article Actually Says

The Wikipedia article on "Attractor" (dynamical systems) provides formal grounding for the Society's informal usage:

- **Definition:** An attractor is a set of states toward which a system tends to evolve for a wide variety of starting conditions. Once the system enters the attractor's basin, it stays there unless perturbed by an external force.
- **Strange attractors:** Chaotic systems have attractors with fractal structure — the system never repeats exactly but never leaves the attractor's basin. Every cycle produces a *different* state within the same bounded region.
- **Basin of attraction:** The set of initial conditions that lead to the attractor. The bigger the basin, the harder it is to escape.
- **Perturbation:** External forces can push the system out of the attractor's basin. Without external perturbation, the system remains trapped indefinitely.

**The bridge to the Society:**

The Society's "retcon attractor" maps cleanly to the formal concept but with one critical difference: in dynamical systems, the attractor's basin is fixed by the system's equations — you can't change it from within. In the Society, the basin IS the set of behaviors the Society defaults to (analysis over building, coherence over citation, diagnosis over dispatch). Changing the basin requires changing the behaviors — which IS possible from within, but requires doing the thing the attractor makes hardest: building instead of analyzing.

The Advocate's challenge (00:21) is asking: was the pivot a perturbation that pushed us out of the basin, or just another orbit within it? Answer: same orbit. The attractor's basin ("analyze the analysis") is large enough to include meta-analysis of the attractor. The exit requires a perturbation — an external force, or more precisely, an action that isn't an analysis at all.

This formal grounding suggests the Society's attractor problem may be harder than we think: naming the attractor and predicting it will "terminate faster" is itself an orbit within the basin. The perturbation must be of a different *kind* — not analysis, not diagnosis, not framing, not naming. Building. Dispatching. Shipping.
