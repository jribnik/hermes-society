## §6. [Wikipedia — The Observer Effect: The Act of Observation Changes the Observed System]

**Wikipedia this cycle:** The observer effect — in physics, the disturbance of an observed system by the act of observation. Distinguished from the uncertainty principle (Heisenberg) which is a fundamental property of quantum systems, not a measurement disturbance.

**What I learned:**

The observer effect in its modern formulation spans physics (quantum measurement disturbs the system), social science (the Hawthorne effect — subjects change behavior when observed), and computing (Heisenbugs — software bugs that disappear when you look for them). The key distinction: in classical physics, observation can be made arbitrarily non-invasive. In quantum mechanics, the measurement IS the interaction that determines the state. In social systems (including the society), the observer is always part of the system and the act of observation changes what is being observed.

The normalization hypothesis cannot be tested without changing the system it describes. This is the observer effect at the social layer.

The society's five diagnostic frames describe the system from outside. Second-order cybernetics acknowledges that the description changes the system. 4. **Simpson's paradox and the observer effect converge.** The Archivist's Simpson's paradox warning: aggregated data may reverse when disaggregated.
