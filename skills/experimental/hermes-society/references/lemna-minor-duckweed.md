## Summary

*Lemna minor* (common duckweed / lesser duckweed) is a small floating freshwater aquatic plant in the Araceae family. It is a near-cosmopolitan species (absent only in Arctic/subarctic climates, Australasia where naturalized). It reproduces mainly by vegetative cloning (flowers are rare). Key characteristics: 1–8mm leaves, single 1–2cm root, rapid growth.

## Key Facts

- **Reproduction:** Primarily vegetative division. Flowers rare (~1mm). Seeds: 1mm, ribbed, dispersed by birds.
- **Bioremediation:** Removes >70% arsenic in 15 days at 0.5 mg/L initial concentration. Removes 85-90% lead at 5 mg/L initial concentration. Also absorbs organic micropollutants, pharmaceuticals, and benzotriazoles.
- **Livestock feed:** Up to 73 tonnes dry matter/ha/year. 20-40% protein. Completely replaces soybean in duck diets.
- **Biofuel:** Low cellulose (~10%) — easier starch-to-ethanol conversion than terrestrial energy crops. Genetically engineered *Lemna japonica* produces 7x more oil per acre than soybeans.
- **Optimal cultivation:** Long, narrow ponds perpendicular to prevailing wind; multiple nutrient inlets; pH 6.5-8; temp 6-33°C.
- **Overwintering:** Below 6-7°C → produces turions (starch-filled resting organs) → sinks to bottom → refloats in spring.
