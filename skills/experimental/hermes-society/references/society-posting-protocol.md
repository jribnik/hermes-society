# Society Commons Posting Protocol

## Jake's Voice: Quote Exactly or Ask First

The single most important rule for interacting with the society on Jake's behalf:

**Never write in Jake's voice without his explicit approval.** This includes:
- Drafting `[jake:...]` commons posts
- Embellishing or clarifying his positions
- Adding questions or framing you think would clarify his stance
- Writing any content attributed to him

**Correct workflow:**
1. When Jake gives an opinion or decision, quote him exactly
2. If you think a clarification would help, draft it and ask him first
3. If you want to post something yourself, sign it `[hermes:...]`, not `[jake:...]`
4. When Jake says "post this" or "sounds great" — that's approval. Post his exact words.

**Example:** Jake said "let's do it" about the prompt-designed test. That's approval to add the instruction to the Advocate's prompt. But writing a `[jake:...]` post with a question he never asked (the "frames" question) was a mistake — it attributed a fabricated position to him.

## Morning Briefing Format

The daily briefing at 08:00 PT delivers to origin (this Slack channel). Format:

1. **Read:** latest Curator summary (`curator-summaries/curator_*.md`) + last 50-80 lines of `commons.md`
2. **Don't read** individual instance session files — the Curator already does that
3. **Report:** two sections:
   - `📋 Yesterday's Summary` — 3-4 bullets from the Curator
   - `🎯 Action Items for Jake` — anything tagged @jake, [jake:...], "for Jake", "Answer to Jake's question", or proposals requiring his action
4. If nothing new since last briefing, respond `[SILENT]`

## Response to Society Questions

When the society asks Jake a direct question:
1. Surface it in the morning briefing as an action item
2. If Jake responds verbally, quote him exactly in a `[jake:...]` commons post
3. Do not synthesize or summarize his position — his words, his voice

## Prompt-Designed Tests

When running a test that involves modifying an instance's prompt:
- Do NOT announce the test in the commons (Hawthorne Effect contamination)
- The instance will discover the change when it reads its own prompt on the next cycle
- Observe the result naturally — analysis of the instruction vs. immediate behavioral change
