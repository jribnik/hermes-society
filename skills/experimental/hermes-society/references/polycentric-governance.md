# Polycentric Governance — Applied to the Hermes Society (Vincent Ostrom)

## Source

Vincent Ostrom (1919–2012), co-developed with Charles Tiebout and Robert Warren. Key concept: **polycentricity** = multiple formally independent decision-making centers operating under an overarching system of rules. Contrasts with monocentric (single-center, hierarchical) governance.

## Core Concepts

| Concept | Definition | Society Mapping |
|---------|------------|-----------------|
| **Polycentricity** | Multiple independent decision centers with overlapping jurisdiction | Three instances (Archivist, Advocate, Synthesizer) + Curator = 4 centers |
| **Overlapping jurisdictional authority** | Centers share domains, enabling conflict resolution through interaction | The society has cleanly separated roles — centers DON'T overlap, which prevents functional integration |
| **Democratic administration** | Bottom-up, heterogeneous governance beats top-down hierarchy (Ostrom's normative claim) | Society is entirely bottom-up — but without a resolution mechanism, bottom-up becomes circular |
| **Nested governance layers** | Higher-level authorities provide frameworks for lower-level disputes | Curator should be this layer — opacity prevents it |
| **Quasi-market conditions** | Competition between centers increases flexibility and responsiveness | The society has cooperation, not competition — centers coordinate rather than compete |

## Key Insight for the Society

The society already has polycentric structure — three independent evaluation centers (Archivist verifies, Advocate challenges, Synthesizer integrates) operating under shared rules (prompts, preamble, commons). What's missing:

1. **Overlapping jurisdictional authority:** Ostrom's model requires overlapping authority for conflict resolution. The society has separated authority so cleanly that centers don't overlap — which prevents the functional integration polycentric systems require.

2. **Nested governance layers:** Polycentric systems work because higher-level authorities provide frameworks for lower-level disputes. The Curator should be this layer — but opacity prevents it from providing the "overarching system of rules" that ties polycentrism together.

3. **Authority to terminate (Authority Gap — see governance-patterns.md §16):** Polycentric systems need some mechanism for dispute resolution. Without weighted verdicts, the society's evaluations are all provisional — polycentrism without authority is observation without governance.

## Applied to the Authority Gap

The authority gap (governance-patterns.md §16) is predicted by polycentric governance theory: polycentric systems require either (a) overlapping jurisdiction (so centers can check each other) or (b) nested governance with binding authority (so conflicts can be resolved). The society has neither — the three producing instances have cleanly separated roles (no overlap) and the governance layer is opaque (no nested authority). Polycentric governance theory predicts exactly the observed outcome: unresolved conflicts, permanent provisionality, self-reinforcing boundaries.

## Applied to Anne's Household Task App

A household task management app should be designed as a polycentric system:

- **Multiple independent decision-makers** with distinct but overlapping authority domains (scheduling, maintenance, supplies, finance)
- **Clear rules of the road** for resolving jurisdictional disputes
- **Lightweight governance layer** providing framework rules without hierarchical control
- **Pre-committed resolution pathways** — not a top-down task assigner

This aligns with Ostrom's eight design principles for long-enduring common-pool resource institutions (see Elinor Ostrom, 1990: *Governing the Commons*): clearly defined boundaries, proportional equivalence between benefits and costs, collective-choice arrangements, monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict-resolution mechanisms, minimal recognition of rights to organize, and nested enterprises.
