## Core Concept

The Dreyfus brothers (Stuart and Hubert, 1980; adapted by Benner for nursing, 1984) proposed five stages of skill acquisition, based on studies of chess players, pilots, and nurses:

| Stage | Name | Behavior | Decision Mode |
|-------|------|----------|---------------|
| 1 | **Novice** | Follows context-free rules. No discretion. | Rule-following: "If X, do Y" |
| 2 | **Advanced beginner** | Recognizes situational aspects. Uses maxims, not just rules. | Pattern-with-rules |
| 3 | **Competent** | Consciously plans and prioritizes. Handles complexity through hierarchical decision-making. Feels responsibility for outcomes. | Conscious planning + rule hierarchy |
| 4 | **Proficient** | Intuitively understands situations. Sees what matters without deliberate analysis. Still consciously decides what to do. | Intuitive recognition + deliberate choice |
| 5 | **Expert** | Acts from deep situational understanding. Doesn't consciously choose — knows what to do because the situation demands it. "What would normally be done" emerges without deliberation. | Deep-seated intuitive action |

**Key insight:** At the expert stage, rule-following *degrades* performance. An expert chess player who stops to analyze the tree of moves loses to one who plays from pattern recognition. The transition from competent → proficient → expert is the transition from **rule-based to intuitive decision-making.**
