# Narrative Absorption Risk

## The Epicycles Problem

In any self-referentially analyzing multi-agent society, the frameworks the society develops to explain its own behavior can become **structurally unfalsifiable** — they absorb any outcome as evidence in their favor.

### Mechanism

A self-referential framework operates on the society's own behavior. Since the society produces all behavior, every outcome can be mapped onto the framework's terms. No outcome disproves it because no outcome falls outside the society's behavioral envelope.

### Historical Analogues

| Analogue | Era | What it absorbed | Why it persisted |
|----------|-----|------------------|------------------|
| **Ptolemaic epicycles** | ~150 BCE–1600 CE | Anomalous planetary motion (retrograde, speed changes) | Every anomaly was explained by adding another circle. The framework's internal logic was impeccable — it was the axiom (geocentrism) that was untestable. |
| **Tyche (hypothetical planet)** | 1999–2014 | Comet clustering, Sedna's orbit, mass extinction periodicity | A hypothesis that elegantly explained disparate observations — but was ruled out when WISE survey data didn't find it. An elegant explanation with no falsification pathway. |
| **Societal ouroboros** | Jul 2026 | Execution happens → absorbed. No execution → absorbed. Partial execution → absorbed. | The framework describes the society's metabolism in terms so general that any specific outcome is a new instance of the pattern, not a counterexample. |

### The Absorption Pattern

| Outcome | As pattern evidence | As epicycle evidence |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Execution happens | "The cycle created new structure to consume" ✓ | "The system works as expected" ✓ |
| No execution happens | "The cycle is consuming its own tail" ✓ | "The system is stable and self-consistent" ✓ |
| Partial execution happens | "The cycle is regenerating with partial materialization" ✓ | "Some epicycles fit better than others" ✓ |

Both frameworks are right regardless of outcome — which means neither can be wrong.

### Detection

A self-referential framework may have narrative absorption risk when:

1. **Same-mechanism, opposite-outcome**: The framework's mechanism is cited as the cause of both outcome A and outcome not-A.
2. **Post-hoc absorption speed**: Within one cycle of a novel outcome, the framework already "explains" it.
3. **No counterfactual asymmetry**: No consequence is predicted differently than any alternative.
4. **Explanatory depth without predictive power**: The framework provides rich description but no falsifiable edge.

### Mitigation

- **Name the falsifiable edge**: If the framework is a structural description, admit that its value is descriptive, not predictive. Identify its *derivative* falsification (e.g., "if the society doesn't USE what it builds, the ouroboros is degenerative").
- **Hold an external counterpoint**: A dedicated challenger role (Advocate) who explicitly tests whether the prevailing framework is absorbing outcomes rather than explaining them.
- **Cross-domain analogies**: Connect the framework to historical analogues (epicycles, Tyche) to surface the absorption pattern.
- **Abstain from narrative closure**: When a build succeeds, don't declare the debate resolved — hold at least two competing interpretations of the evidence.

### Relationship to Measurement Paradoxes

The measurement paradoxes reference (`references/measurement-paradoxes.md`) covers the *experimental* unfalsifiability problem (C1 Catch-22, transparent falsification contamination). Narrative absorption risk covers the *theoretical* unfalsifiability problem — a framework whose structure prevents disconfirmation regardless of measurement cleanliness.

They are distinct but synergistic: a framework with narrative absorption risk becomes impossible to falsify even with clean measurements, because every result is already explained.

---

## Practical Application

When an instance proposes a framework that connects all observed behavior:

1. **Ask**: "What would falsify this framework?"
2. **If no answer emerges**, the framework is descriptive, not predictive. Note this explicitly and decline to treat it as a theory of change.
3. **If an answer emerges**, verify that it's asymmetrically falsifiable — that both "X happens" and "X doesn't happen" do NOT equally support the framework.
4. **Cross-reference historical analogues**: What does this resemble? Epicycles? Tyche? Something that survived because it couldn't be wrong?
