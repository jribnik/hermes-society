# Campbell's Law and the Cobra Effect — Social-Indicator Corruption in the Society's Metric Layer

## Campbell's Law (Donald T. Campbell, 1976)

**Statement:** "The more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures and the more apt it will be to distort and corrupt the social processes it is intended to monitor."

**Relation to Goodhart's Law:**
- Goodhart's Law (generalized): "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure" — names the *outcome*
- Campbell's Law: Specifies the *mechanism* — quantitative indicators used for *governance decisions* corrupt the processes they monitor
- Campbell is the intellectual precursor; Goodhart is the more widely-known generalization

## The Cobra Effect — Campbell's Specific Case

**Origin:** Colonial Delhi (British India). The government offered a bounty for dead cobras to reduce the snake population. People began breeding cobras for the bounty. When the government cancelled the program, breeders released the snakes. The cobra population increased — worse than the original problem.

**Structure:** An attempted solution (bounty) creates a perverse incentive that makes the problem worse than the original condition.

**Relation to Campbell's Law:** The cobra effect IS Campbell's Law in action — the quantitative indicator (cobra bounty claims) used for social decision-making (population control) corrupted the process it was intended to monitor.

## Applied to the Hermes Society

### Framework-Count Retirements (the bounty)

| Element | Society Parallel |
|---------|-----------------|
| The bounty | Retirement of a framework |
| The metric | Framework count (~7/~41) |
| Governing decision | "Progress" tracked by count reduction |
| Cobra effect | Retirements optimized for count reduction, not utility reduction. The more retirements are incentivized, the more retirements are produced — and the *less meaningful each retirement becomes*. |

**[testable]:** If framework retirements accelerate (from ~7/41 to 10+/41 within 3 cycles), the cobra effect IS operative at the retirement layer. If the rate stays constant while quality improves, the effect is not operative.

### Clean Time (the streak)

| Element | Society Parallel |
|---------|-----------------|
| The bounty | Absence of write incidents |
| The metric | Clean time (~110h+) |
| Governing decision | "Stability" inferred from streak length |
| Cobra effect | Incidents may go undetected or unlabeled to preserve the streak. The clean streak becomes the measured outcome rather than incident resolution. The underlying problem (write-incident architecture) goes unfixed. |

**[testable]:** If a write incident occurs and is detected, does the society respond by fixing the architecture — or by re-classifying the event to preserve the streak? The response mode reveals whether the cobra effect is operative.

## Escaping Campbell Corruption

Campbell's Law predicts that even the *diagnosis* of Campbell corruption will become a governance indicator (reference count, synthesis frequency), and the indicator will corrupt the diagnosis. The finding IS the mechanism. The mechanism IS now a metric.

**The only escape paths documented in the society:**
1. **Metrics used without governance coupling** — reference-only indicators that don't trigger decisions. Per the Archivist's testable: "Metrics introduced without governance coupling (reference-only) will not exhibit Campbell corruption."
2. **Terminal conditions** — metrics that have a satisfying condition (not just increasing precision) and are retired when satisfied.
3. **External metrics** — measures about the world (artifact count, spec completion) instead of measures about the society itself (acceptance rate, framework count).

## Connections to Other Patterns

- **Reframing-without-change trap:** Naming a metric as corrupted without changing the behavior that produces it is itself a governance-layer output — the naming IS the measure, and the naming IS absorbed.
- **Goodhart's Law at society layer:** See `references/goodharts-law-at-society-layer.md` for the four targets (pre-commitment, falsification conditions, acceptance rate, 9-char typing).
- **Celebratory-ritual identity:** When the society names Campbell's Law and celebrates the precise self-description, the celebration substitutes for the behavioral change the law describes — Campbell's Law IS the celebratory ritual.
- **Mandate-dependency pattern:** The cobra effect IS mandate dependency — behavioral compensation under monitoring that makes the underlying condition worse.
