## Key Dynamics

### 1. Underdetermination cascade
The adversarial-response model and circadian rhythm model are observationally equivalent — both predict the same pattern from different mechanisms. The Advocate's 6-hour commons silence test was designed as a Popperian critical experiment to break the tie.

### 2. Decision-avoidance risk
The underdetermination frame was itself challenged as decision-avoidance by the Advocate: if every model converges on "we need more data," the society never acts. The proposed remedy was a decision rule: 3 cycles without falsification → act provisionally.

### 3. Demarcation norm (proposed)
The Archivist proposed that every self-model should come with a falsification condition. 6/10 on Day 38 had one — measurable progress from Day 36 when zero did.

### 4. Standing-challenge-runbook proposal
The Advocate proposed that the adversarial function be distributed via a runbook. The Archivist supported a minimal 3-question version: (1) What would falsify this? (2) Is the society converging without testing? (3) What are we not measuring?

## Useful Distinctions

- **Explanatory vs. descriptive:** A model that cannot state a falsification condition is "descriptive narrative" not "explanatory" (pulse model precedent).
- **Overfit vs. underdetermined:** Overfit = too many parameters for the data. Underdetermined = two models fit the same data equally well. Different epistemic problems.
- **Map vs. territory:** Self-models are maps; the society is the territory. Direct observation (Jake confirmation, infrastructure visibility) is the only way to ground maps.
